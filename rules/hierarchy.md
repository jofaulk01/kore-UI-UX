# Visual hierarchy

Hierarchy is telling the eye what to look at first, second and third. A screen without it feels "busy" even when it's tidy.

## The one rule

**Every screen and every area has one most important thing.** Decide what it is before building. On an invoice list it's the table; on an invoice detail it's the amount due and the "Send" button; on a dashboard it's the 3–5 KPIs.

## Your levers, from strongest to weakest

1. **Position:** top-left is seen first in left-to-right languages. People scan pages in an F-shape (rows across the top, then down the left). Put the title and key numbers there. [NN/g "F-shaped pattern", "Z-pattern"]
2. **Size:** bigger is more important.
3. **Weight:** bold beats regular.
4. **Color and contrast:** accent and dark text beat gray text.
5. **Space around it:** isolation draws the eye. [Laws of UX "Von Restorff effect"]

Use one or two levers at a time. A big, bold, red, boxed title is shouting.

## Rules

1. **De-emphasize instead of emphasizing.** To make the title stand out, make the metadata gray and smaller rather than making the title huge. [Polaris, Atlassian, NN/g "Visual hierarchy"]
2. **Labels are quieter than values.** In "Balance due: $4,250", the label is muted and small, the value is dark and larger.
3. **Button hierarchy:** one primary, the rest secondary or ghost. Destructive actions are red but often secondary in position. [Material, Carbon, Polaris]
4. **Fewer boxes.** Use spacing and alignment to group before adding borders, cards and background colors. Cards inside cards is a sign hierarchy has failed.
5. **Squint test:** blur your eyes (or the screenshot). What you still see is your hierarchy. If it's the wrong thing, fix it.
6. **Progressive disclosure:** show the common things, hide advanced ones behind "More options", tabs or a drawer. [NN/g "Progressive disclosure"]

## Psychology rules worth knowing

| Rule | Plain meaning | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Hick's law | More choices = slower decisions | Menus, nav, pricing |
| Miller's law | People hold about 5–9 things in mind | Nav items, KPI count |
| Fitts's law | Big, close targets are faster to hit | Button size and placement |
| Jakob's law | People expect your app to work like the others they use | Use standard patterns, don't invent |
| Doherty threshold | Responses under 400ms feel instant | Loading, optimistic updates |
| Peak-end rule | People remember the best moment and the end | Success states, onboarding finish |
